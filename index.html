02 · The shift

The cover is colorful because the color is boxed.

The art of co-design does not paint the whole page. Cream stays cream. Type stays near-black. Every bright hue lives inside a square. That is the rule to borrow — not a rainbow background.

1

Keep the cream field

The book’s off-white is doing the calming work. Star Therapy should do the same with #F7F3EC.

2

Give each service a tile color

Speech gets coral. Occupational therapy gets cobalt. Parents learn the code without reading a legend.

3

Use mustard as warmth, not warning

Team, highlights, and small “you are here” marks. Never body text.

4

One action color

Poppy red is the only button color, matching the cover’s loudest tile. Everything else is identity, not conversion.

Creative director’s note

Match the book’s structure, not its subject matter. Quiet page. Loud squares. Friendly type.

Bronze stays for the star mark so the logo still feels like Star Therapy, not a children’s textbook.
03 · Essence

Warm, clear, a little playful.

Warm
Clear
Playful
Parents are the reader

Color can be cheerful. Copy stays plain: what we do, where we go, how to start.

One job per tile

A color means a category. Speech, OT, school visits, insurance, team. Do not remix them inside one card.

Squares for color, pills for action

Tiles and icon chips hold the palette. Buttons stay fully rounded so they never look like another tile.

Clinical, not clinical-looking

This is speech pathology and occupational therapy. Skip baby clip-art. Use real rooms, real hands, real play.
